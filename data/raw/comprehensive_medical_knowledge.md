## Respiratory and Infectious Diseases

### Influenza (Flu)

#### General Information
Influenza is a viral infection that attacks the respiratory system — nose, throat and lungs. It is caused by influenza viruses and can range from mild to severe.

#### Common Symptoms
- High fever (often sudden onset)
- Muscle aches and joint pain
- Chills and sweats
- Headache
- Dry, persistent cough
- Fatigue and weakness
- Nasal congestion
- Sore throat

#### Possible Conditions
Symptoms of influenza overlap with:
- COVID-19
- Common cold
- Acute bronchitis
- Pneumonia

#### Recommended Care
- Rest extensively and sleep.
- Drink plenty of liquids to prevent dehydration.
- Use over-the-counter pain relievers (e.g., acetaminophen or ibuprofen) to manage fever and aches.
- Antiviral medications may be prescribed if caught early.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek urgent medical care if experiencing:
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Chest pain
- Ongoing dizziness or confusion
- Seizures
- Severe muscle pain
- Severe weakness or unsteadiness

### Strep Throat

#### General Information
Strep throat is a bacterial infection (Group A Streptococcus) that causes a severe and sudden sore throat. It is highly contagious.

#### Common Symptoms
- Severe and sudden throat pain
- Pain when swallowing
- Red and swollen tonsils, sometimes with white patches or streaks of pus
- Tiny, red spots on the roof of the mouth
- Swollen, tender lymph nodes in the neck
- Fever

#### Possible Conditions
- Streptococcal pharyngitis (Strep throat)
- Viral pharyngitis (Common cold sore throat)
- Tonsillitis
- Mononucleosis

#### Recommended Care
- Requires a medical diagnosis (throat swab).
- Prescription antibiotics (e.g., penicillin or amoxicillin) are usually required.
- Rest and stay hydrated.
- Consume soothing foods (warm liquids, cold foods).
- Over-the-counter pain relievers for throat pain.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek urgent medical attention if:
- Difficulty breathing or swallowing
- Drooling (unable to swallow saliva)
- Swelling of the neck or tongue
- High fever persisting over 48 hours
- Joint pain or a rash develops

## Gastrointestinal Issues

### Gastroenteritis (Stomach Flu)

#### General Information
Viral gastroenteritis is an intestinal infection that includes watery diarrhea, abdominal cramps, nausea or vomiting, and sometimes fever. It is commonly called "stomach flu," though it is not related to influenza.

#### Common Symptoms
- Watery, usually non-bloody diarrhea
- Abdominal cramps and pain
- Nausea, vomiting, or both
- Occasional muscle aches or headache
- Low-grade fever

#### Possible Conditions
- Viral gastroenteritis (Norovirus, Rotavirus)
- Food poisoning
- Bacterial gastroenteritis (Salmonella, E. coli)
- Irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) flare-up

#### Recommended Care
- Let the stomach settle by stopping eating solid foods for a few hours.
- Suck on ice chips or take small sips of water.
- Drink clear broths or non-caffeinated sports drinks to stay hydrated.
- Ease back into eating with bland, easy-to-digest foods (crackers, toast, gelatin, bananas).
- Avoid dairy, caffeine, alcohol, and fatty or highly seasoned foods.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek urgent medical care if you experience:
- Inability to keep liquids down for 24 hours
- Vomiting blood
- Bloody bowel movements
- Signs of severe dehydration (excessive thirst, dry mouth, little to no urination, severe weakness)
- A fever above 104 F (40 C)

### Acid Reflux (GERD)

#### General Information
Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) occurs when stomach acid frequently flows back into the tube connecting the mouth and stomach (esophagus). This backwash (acid reflux) can irritate the lining of the esophagus.

#### Common Symptoms
- A burning sensation in the chest (heartburn), usually after eating, which might be worse at night
- Chest pain
- Difficulty swallowing (dysphagia)
- Regurgitation of food or sour liquid
- Sensation of a lump in your throat
- Chronic cough or laryngitis

#### Possible Conditions
- Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)
- Peptic ulcer disease
- Gastritis
- Esophagitis
- Angina (Heart pain) - *Must be ruled out*

#### Recommended Care
- Avoid trigger foods (spicy foods, tomatoes, chocolate, mint, garlic, onions, caffeine).
- Eat smaller meals and do not eat within 3 hours of bedtime.
- Elevate the head of the bed.
- Over-the-counter antacids (Tums, Rolaids) or H2 blockers.
- Maintain a healthy weight.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek immediate emergency medical attention if:
- Chest pain radiates to the arm, neck, or jaw (this can indicate a heart attack, not just reflux).
- You experience shortness of breath along with chest pain.
- You vomit blood or material that looks like coffee grounds.
- You have bloody or black, tarry stools.
- Severe difficulty swallowing or feeling like food is stuck in your throat.

## Cardiovascular and Neurological

### Migraine

#### General Information
A migraine is a neurological condition that can cause multiple symptoms, most notably a throbbing, pulsing headache on one side of the head.

#### Common Symptoms
- Pain usually on one side of the head, but often on both sides
- Pain that throbs or pulses
- Sensitivity to light, sound, and sometimes smell and touch
- Nausea and vomiting
- Visual disturbances (aura), such as seeing flashes of light or blind spots (before or during the headache)

#### Possible Conditions
- Migraine
- Tension headache
- Cluster headache
- Sinus headache

#### Recommended Care
- Rest in a quiet, dark room.
- Apply a cold compress or ice pack to the forehead or back of the neck.
- Drink fluids.
- Take over-the-counter pain relievers (ibuprofen, aspirin, acetaminophen) at the onset of symptoms.
- Prescription medications (triptans) may be required for severe cases.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek urgent emergency medical care for:
- An abrupt, severe headache like a thunderclap
- Headache with fever, stiff neck, confusion, seizures, double vision, numbness or weakness in any part of the body (signs of stroke or meningitis)
- Headache following a head injury
- A chronic headache that is worse after coughing, exertion, straining or a sudden movement

### Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) & Cardiovascular Warning

#### General Information
High blood pressure is a common condition in which the long-term force of the blood against your artery walls is high enough that it may eventually cause health problems, such as heart disease. It is often called a "silent killer" because it usually has no symptoms until significant damage is done.

#### Common Symptoms
- Most people have no symptoms.
- In rare, extremely severe cases (hypertensive crisis): Headaches, shortness of breath, or nosebleeds.

#### Possible Conditions
- Essential (primary) hypertension
- Secondary hypertension (due to kidney disease, thyroid issues, etc.)
- Impending cardiovascular events

#### Recommended Care
- Requires medical diagnosis through blood pressure monitoring.
- Lifestyle changes: reduced sodium diet, regular exercise, weight management, limiting alcohol, quitting smoking.
- Prescription antihypertensive medications (ACE inhibitors, beta-blockers, diuretics) as directed by a doctor.

#### Critical Warning Signs (Heart Attack / Stroke)
Seek IMMEDIATE emergency medical attention (Call 911) if experiencing:
- Chest pain, pressure, fullness, or squeezing (Heart Attack)
- Pain radiating to the arms, neck, jaw, or back (Heart Attack)
- Sudden numbness or weakness in the face, arm, or leg, especially on one side (Stroke)
- Sudden confusion, trouble speaking, or difficulty understanding speech (Stroke)
- Sudden trouble seeing in one or both eyes (Stroke)
- Sudden trouble walking, dizziness, loss of balance, or lack of coordination (Stroke)

## Dermatological and Allergic

### Contact Dermatitis & Allergic Reactions

#### General Information
Contact dermatitis is a red, itchy rash caused by direct contact with a substance or an allergic reaction to it. The rash isn't contagious or life-threatening, but it can be very uncomfortable.

#### Common Symptoms
- A red rash
- Itching, which may be severe
- Dry, cracked, scaly skin
- Bumps and blisters, sometimes with oozing and crusting
- Swelling, burning or tenderness

#### Possible Conditions
- Allergic contact dermatitis (poison ivy, nickel, cosmetics)
- Irritant contact dermatitis (soaps, bleach, sanitizers)
- Atopic dermatitis (Eczema)
- Hives (Urticaria)

#### Recommended Care
- Avoid the irritant or allergen.
- Wash the skin with mild soap and cool water.
- Apply a cool, wet compress.
- Apply over-the-counter anti-itch cream (hydrocortisone) or calamine lotion.
- Take oral antihistamines to reduce itching.

#### Critical Warning Signs (Anaphylaxis)
Seek IMMEDIATE emergency medical care if a rash or exposure is accompanied by:
- Swelling of the face, lips, tongue, or throat
- Difficulty breathing, wheezing, or shortness of breath
- Dizziness, fainting, or loss of consciousness
- A rapid, weak pulse
- Nausea and vomiting combined with other systemic symptoms

## Musculoskeletal Issues

### Osteoarthritis

#### General Information
Osteoarthritis is the most common form of arthritis, occurring when the protective cartilage that cushions the ends of the bones wears down over time. It most commonly affects joints in your hands, knees, hips and spine.

#### Common Symptoms
- Pain in affected joints during or after movement
- Stiffness, especially upon awakening or after being inactive
- Tenderness when applying light pressure to or near the joint
- Loss of flexibility
- Grating sensation or popping/crackling sound when using the joint
- Bone spurs (extra bits of bone) around the affected joint

#### Possible Conditions
- Osteoarthritis
- Rheumatoid Arthritis
- Gout
- Tendinitis
- Bursitis

#### Recommended Care
- Stay active and exercise to strengthen muscles around the joints.
- Maintain a healthy weight to reduce stress on weight-bearing joints.
- Apply heat (for stiffness) or cold (for muscle spasms/pain).
- Over-the-counter pain relievers (acetaminophen, ibuprofen) or topical creams.
- Physical therapy.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek medical evaluation if you experience:
- Joint pain that completely prevents you from moving
- A joint that is suddenly hot to the touch, red, or significantly swollen (could indicate infection or gout)
- Fever accompanied by joint pain

### Rheumatoid Arthritis

#### General Information
Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic inflammatory disorder that can affect more than just your joints. An autoimmune condition, it occurs when your immune system mistakenly attacks your own body's tissues.

#### Common Symptoms
- Tender, warm, swollen joints
- Joint stiffness that is usually worse in the mornings and after inactivity
- Fatigue, fever and loss of appetite
- Symmetrical symptom presentation (e.g., both hands or both knees)

#### Possible Conditions
- Rheumatoid Arthritis
- Lupus
- Osteoarthritis
- Psoriatic Arthritis

#### Recommended Care
- Early diagnosis and treatment by a rheumatologist is critical.
- Disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (DMARDs) or biologic agents.
- NSAIDs and corticosteroids to reduce inflammation.
- Rest during flare-ups and physical therapy.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek urgent medical care if:
- Sudden, severe joint swelling accompanied by fever (possible septic joint)
- Difficulty breathing or chest pain (RA can affect the lungs and heart)

## Endocrine System

### Type 2 Diabetes

#### General Information
Type 2 diabetes is an impairment in the way the body regulates and uses sugar (glucose) as a fuel. This long-term (chronic) condition results in too much sugar circulating in the bloodstream.

#### Common Symptoms
- Increased thirst
- Frequent urination
- Increased hunger
- Unintended weight loss
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing sores
- Frequent infections
- Numbness or tingling in the hands or feet

#### Possible Conditions
- Type 2 Diabetes
- Type 1 Diabetes
- Prediabetes
- Hyperthyroidism (weight loss, fatigue)

#### Recommended Care
- Lifestyle changes: healthy eating, regular physical activity, weight loss.
- Blood sugar monitoring.
- Oral diabetes medications or insulin therapy as prescribed.
- Regular checkups for foot, eye, and kidney health.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek immediate emergency medical attention if you experience signs of extreme blood sugar imbalances:
- Very high blood sugar (Hyperglycemia): Fruity-smelling breath, nausea and vomiting, shortness of breath, dry mouth, weakness, confusion, coma (Diabetic ketoacidosis).
- Very low blood sugar (Hypoglycemia): Shakiness, sweating, chills, irritability, fast heartbeat, confusion, seizures, loss of consciousness.

## Additional Respiratory

### Asthma

#### General Information
Asthma is a condition in which your airways narrow and swell and may produce extra mucus. This can make breathing difficult and trigger coughing, a whistling sound (wheezing) when you breathe out and shortness of breath.

#### Common Symptoms
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness or pain
- Wheezing when exhaling (common in children)
- Trouble sleeping caused by shortness of breath, coughing or wheezing
- Coughing or wheezing attacks that are worsened by a respiratory virus

#### Possible Conditions
- Asthma
- Bronchitis
- COPD
- Allergies
- Heart failure (in older adults)

#### Recommended Care
- Use of a prescribed quick-relief inhaler (albuterol) during attacks.
- Long-term control medications (inhaled corticosteroids) for daily prevention.
- Identifying and avoiding asthma triggers (allergens, cold air, exercise, stress).
- Monitoring peak flow to measure lung function.

#### Critical Warning Signs (Severe Asthma Attack)
Seek IMMEDIATE emergency medical care if experiencing:
- Rapid worsening of shortness of breath or wheezing
- No improvement even after using a quick-relief inhaler
- Shortness of breath when doing minimal physical activity
- Bluish tint to the lips or face (cyanosis)
- Extreme difficulty talking or walking due to breathlessness

## Urinary System

### Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

#### General Information
A urinary tract infection (UTI) is an infection in any part of your urinary system — your kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra. Most infections involve the lower urinary tract — the bladder and the urethra.

#### Common Symptoms
- A strong, persistent urge to urinate
- A burning sensation when urinating
- Passing frequent, small amounts of urine
- Urine that appears cloudy
- Urine that appears red, bright pink or cola-colored — a sign of blood in the urine
- Strong-smelling urine
- Pelvic pain, in women — especially in the center of the pelvis and around the area of the pubic bone

#### Possible Conditions
- Cystitis (Bladder infection)
- Urethritis (Infection of the urethra)
- Pyelonephritis (Kidney infection)
- Sexually transmitted infections (STIs)
- Kidney stones

#### Recommended Care
- Requires medical diagnosis and a prescription of antibiotics.
- Drink plenty of water to help flush bacteria from the urinary tract.
- Avoid drinks that may irritate the bladder (coffee, alcohol, soft drinks containing citrus juices or caffeine).
- Use a heating pad on your abdomen to minimize bladder pressure or discomfort.

#### Critical Warning Signs (Kidney Infection / Sepsis)
Seek urgent medical care if you experience:
- Upper back and side (flank) pain
- High fever
- Shaking and chills
- Nausea and vomiting
- Confusion or disorientation (especially in older adults)

## More Conditions

### Appendicitis

#### General Information
Appendicitis is an inflammation of the appendix, a finger-shaped pouch that projects from your colon on the lower right side of your abdomen.

#### Common Symptoms
- Sudden pain that begins on the right side of the lower abdomen
- Sudden pain that begins around your navel and often shifts to your lower right abdomen
- Pain that worsens if you cough, walk or make other jarring movements
- Nausea and vomiting
- Loss of appetite
- Low-grade fever that may worsen as the illness progresses

#### Possible Conditions
- Appendicitis
- Gastroenteritis
- Ectopic pregnancy
- Ovarian cyst
- Kidney stone

#### Recommended Care
- Requires immediate medical diagnosis (ultrasound, CT scan).
- Treatment usually involves surgery to remove the inflamed appendix (appendectomy).
- Antibiotics are given to prevent or treat infection.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek IMMEDIATE emergency medical care if:
- Abdominal pain is severe and sudden
- You experience severe pain accompanied by a high fever
- Your abdomen is swollen and rigid

### Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

#### General Information
COPD is a chronic inflammatory lung disease that causes obstructed airflow from the lungs. Symptoms include breathing difficulty, cough, mucus (sputum) production and wheezing.

#### Common Symptoms
- Shortness of breath, especially during physical activities
- Wheezing
- Chest tightness
- A chronic cough that may produce mucus (sputum) that may be clear, white, yellow or greenish
- Frequent respiratory infections
- Lack of energy

#### Possible Conditions
- COPD (Emphysema, Chronic Bronchitis)
- Asthma
- Heart Failure
- Lung Cancer

#### Recommended Care
- Smoking cessation is the most critical step.
- Inhaled bronchodilators and corticosteroids.
- Pulmonary rehabilitation.
- Supplemental oxygen therapy in advanced stages.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek urgent medical care if:
- You have severe shortness of breath that doesn't improve with medications
- You experience confusion, lethargy, or extreme fatigue
- Your lips or fingernail beds turn blue (cyanosis)

### Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

#### General Information
Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) occurs when a blood clot (thrombus) forms in one or more of the deep veins in your body, usually in your legs. DVT can cause leg pain or swelling but also can occur with no symptoms.

#### Common Symptoms
- Swelling in the affected leg (rarely swelling in both legs)
- Pain in your leg, which often starts in your calf and can feel like cramping or soreness
- Red or discolored skin on the leg
- A feeling of warmth in the affected leg

#### Possible Conditions
- Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)
- Muscle strain or tear
- Cellulitis
- Superficial thrombophlebitis

#### Recommended Care
- Requires immediate medical diagnosis (ultrasound).
- Blood thinners (anticoagulants) to prevent the clot from getting bigger.
- Clot busters (thrombolytics) in severe cases.
- Wearing compression stockings.

#### Critical Warning Signs (Pulmonary Embolism)
Seek IMMEDIATE emergency medical care if you experience:
- Sudden shortness of breath
- Chest pain or discomfort that worsens when you take a deep breath or when you cough
- Feeling lightheaded or dizzy, or fainting
- Rapid pulse
- Coughing up blood

### Gallstones

#### General Information
Gallstones are hardened deposits of digestive fluid that can form in your gallbladder. Your gallbladder is a small, pear-shaped organ on the right side of your abdomen, just beneath your liver.

#### Common Symptoms
- Sudden and rapidly intensifying pain in the upper right portion of your abdomen
- Sudden and rapidly intensifying pain in the center of your abdomen, just below your breastbone
- Back pain between your shoulder blades
- Pain in your right shoulder
- Nausea or vomiting

#### Possible Conditions
- Gallstones (Cholelithiasis)
- Peptic ulcer
- Pancreatitis
- Appendicitis

#### Recommended Care
- Requires medical diagnosis (ultrasound, CT scan).
- If asymptomatic, careful monitoring.
- If symptomatic, surgery to remove the gallbladder (cholecystectomy) is the most common treatment.
- Medications to dissolve gallstones (rarely used as they take months/years).

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek immediate emergency medical attention if:
- Abdominal pain is so intense that you can't sit still or find a comfortable position
- Yellowing of your skin and the whites of your eyes (jaundice)
- High fever with chills

### Kidney Stones

#### General Information
Kidney stones (renal lithiasis, nephrolithiasis) are hard deposits made of minerals and salts that form inside your kidneys. Passing kidney stones can be quite painful.

#### Common Symptoms
- Severe, sharp pain in the side and back, below the ribs
- Pain that radiates to the lower abdomen and groin
- Pain that comes in waves and fluctuates in intensity
- Pain or burning sensation while urinating
- Pink, red or brown urine
- Cloudy or foul-smelling urine

#### Possible Conditions
- Kidney Stones
- Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)
- Appendicitis (if right-sided)
- Muscle spasm

#### Recommended Care
- Drink plenty of water to help flush out the stone.
- Over-the-counter pain relievers for mild discomfort.
- Medical therapy (alpha blockers) to help pass the stone.
- For large stones, procedures like lithotripsy (sound waves) or surgery may be required.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek immediate medical attention if you experience:
- Pain so severe that you can't sit still
- Pain accompanied by nausea and vomiting
- Pain accompanied by fever and chills (indicates infection)
- Blood in your urine
- Difficulty passing urine

### Lyme Disease

#### General Information
Lyme disease is an illness caused by borrelia bacteria. Humans usually get Lyme disease from the bite of a tick carrying the bacteria.

#### Common Symptoms
- A rash that looks like a bull's-eye (erythema migrans)
- Fever and chills
- Fatigue
- Body aches and joint pain
- Headache
- Swollen lymph nodes

#### Possible Conditions
- Lyme Disease
- Other tick-borne illnesses
- Viral syndrome (flu)
- Cellulitis

#### Recommended Care
- Requires medical diagnosis and early treatment with oral antibiotics (e.g., doxycycline, amoxicillin).
- Rest and pain relievers for joint pain.
- Check for and safely remove any ticks.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek medical care if you develop:
- Severe joint pain and swelling (Lyme arthritis)
- Neurological problems (facial palsy, numbness, impaired muscle movement)
- Heart problems (Lyme carditis - irregular heartbeat)

### Psoriasis

#### General Information
Psoriasis is a skin disease that causes a rash with itchy, scaly patches, most commonly on the knees, elbows, trunk and scalp. It is a common, long-term (chronic) disease with no cure.

#### Common Symptoms
- Red patches of skin covered with thick, silvery scales
- Small scaling spots (commonly seen in children)
- Dry, cracked skin that may bleed or itch
- Itching, burning or soreness
- Thickened, pitted or ridged nails
- Swollen and stiff joints

#### Possible Conditions
- Plaque Psoriasis
- Eczema
- Ringworm
- Seborrheic dermatitis

#### Recommended Care
- Topical treatments (corticosteroids, vitamin D analogues, retinoids).
- Light therapy (phototherapy).
- Oral or injected medications (biologics, methotrexate) for severe cases.
- Use moisturizers daily.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek medical attention if:
- The rash spreads rapidly and becomes severely painful
- You develop sudden fever, chills, and muscle weakness alongside a widespread rash
- Your joints become severely swollen and painful (Psoriatic arthritis)

### Sleep Apnea

#### General Information
Sleep apnea is a potentially serious sleep disorder in which breathing repeatedly stops and starts. The most common type is obstructive sleep apnea.

#### Common Symptoms
- Loud snoring
- Episodes in which you stop breathing during sleep — which would be reported by another person
- Gasping for air during sleep
- Awakening with a dry mouth
- Morning headache
- Difficulty staying asleep (insomnia)
- Excessive daytime sleepiness (hypersomnia)

#### Possible Conditions
- Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA)
- Central Sleep Apnea
- Narcolepsy
- Chronic fatigue syndrome

#### Recommended Care
- Requires a sleep study for definitive diagnosis.
- Continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) machine therapy.
- Weight loss and lifestyle changes.
- Avoid alcohol and sleeping pills.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek medical attention if you or your partner notice:
- You frequently wake up gasping or choking
- You have excessive daytime sleepiness that causes you to fall asleep while working or driving
- You develop high blood pressure or heart problems alongside snoring

### Ulcerative Colitis

#### General Information
Ulcerative colitis is an inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) that causes inflammation and ulcers (sores) in your digestive tract. It affects the innermost lining of your large intestine (colon) and rectum.

#### Common Symptoms
- Diarrhea, often with blood or pus
- Abdominal pain and cramping
- Rectal pain and bleeding
- Urgency to defecate
- Inability to defecate despite urgency
- Weight loss
- Fatigue and fever

#### Possible Conditions
- Ulcerative Colitis
- Crohn's Disease
- Infectious colitis
- Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

#### Recommended Care
- Requires medical diagnosis by a gastroenterologist (colonoscopy).
- Anti-inflammatory drugs (aminosalicylates, corticosteroids).
- Immune system suppressors and biologics.
- In severe cases, surgery to remove the colon (colectomy) may be necessary.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek immediate emergency medical attention if you experience:
- Severe, unrelenting abdominal pain
- Heavy, continuous rectal bleeding
- High fever
- A rapid heart rate

### Shingles (Herpes Zoster)

#### General Information
Shingles is a viral infection that causes a painful rash. It is caused by the varicella-zoster virus — the same virus that causes chickenpox. After you've had chickenpox, the virus lies inactive in nerve tissue near your spinal cord and brain, and years later, it may reactivate as shingles.

#### Common Symptoms
- Pain, burning, numbness or tingling
- Sensitivity to touch
- A red rash that begins a few days after the pain
- Fluid-filled blisters that break open and crust over
- Itching
- Fever, headache, sensitivity to light, and fatigue

#### Possible Conditions
- Shingles
- Contact dermatitis
- Hives
- Cellulitis

#### Recommended Care
- Antiviral drugs (acyclovir, valacyclovir) if started within 72 hours can speed healing and reduce the risk of complications.
- Over-the-counter pain relievers and topical creams (calamine lotion).
- Cool compresses to ease pain and itching.

#### Critical Warning Signs
Seek urgent medical care if:
- The pain and rash occur near an eye (this can cause permanent eye damage)
- You have a weakened immune system
- The rash spreads and becomes extremely painful or looks infected
